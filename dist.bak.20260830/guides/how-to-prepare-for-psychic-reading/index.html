How to Prepare for a Psychic Reading: The Complete Checklist for Better Results

A step-by-step preparation guide for psychic readings -- including 20+ love questions to ask, mindset tips, budget planning, and a 48-hour preparation framework.

This is the guide I wish someone had handed me before my first psychic reading.

I remember sitting at my kitchen table in 2019, phone in hand, about to connect with a reader for the very first time. I had just found out my partner was texting his ex. My hands were shaking. My only plan was to blurt out “is he cheating on me?” and hope for a definitive answer.

That reading cost me $87 and gave me almost nothing useful. Not because the reader was bad — she was actually quite good — but because I walked in with zero preparation, sky-high emotions, and no idea what to actually ask. I essentially paid someone nearly a hundred dollars to absorb my panic.

Since then, I have done well over a hundred readings across multiple platforms. I have spent real money. I have learned — sometimes painfully — what separates a reading that changes your perspective from one that drains your wallet. And the single biggest factor is not the reader. It is how prepared you are before you connect.

This guide is my complete preparation system. Bookmark it. Print it out. Use it as a checklist before every single session. Whether you are wondering what to expect from a psychic reading or you already have ten sessions under your belt, this framework will save you money and get you answers that actually matter.

The 48-Hour Preparation Framework

Most people book a psychic reading the moment they feel desperate. I get it — I have done it too. But the best readings happen when you give yourself a structured runway. Here is my 48-hour framework, broken into three phases.

Phase 1: The Brain Dump (48 Hours Before)

Two days before your reading, sit down with a notebook or your phone’s notes app and do a raw brain dump. Write down everything that is bothering you, every question swirling in your head, every situation you want clarity on. Do not edit yourself. Do not organize. Just get it all out.

This might look like a messy list: “Why did Marcus stop texting? Am I going to get that promotion? Is my mom’s health going to improve? Should I move to Denver? Why do I keep picking the same type of guy?”

The point is not to ask all of these questions. The point is to see what is actually on your mind when you stop filtering yourself. Most people discover that their real question is buried under three layers of surface-level anxiety.

Phase 2: The Refine (24 Hours Before)

The next day, look at your brain dump with fresh eyes. Circle the 3-5 questions that feel most important. Then rewrite each one to be specific and open-ended. I will cover exactly how to do this in the questions section below, but the general rule is: replace “will he” with “what do I need to understand about” and replace vague topics with specific names, dates, and situations.

This is also when you should decide your budget, choose your platform, and pick your reader. Do not leave these decisions for the moment you are logging on — that is when impulse spending happens.

Phase 3: The Reset (30 Minutes Before)

Half an hour before your session, put your phone on silent, close your laptop, and spend 10 minutes doing literally anything calming. A short walk. Deep breathing. A cup of tea with no screen in front of you. The goal is to walk into the reading from a place of genuine curiosity rather than raw desperation.

Have your refined question list in front of you. Have a pen ready for notes. Know your hard spending limit. You are prepared.

20+ Questions to Ask a Psychic About Love (Ranked by Usefulness)

This is the section I needed most before my first reading. Not all questions are created equal — some open doors to real insight, and others are basically conversation dead ends that burn through your minutes. I have ranked these from most useful to least useful, with explanations for each tier.

Tier 1: The Best Questions (Ask These First)

These questions give readers the most to work with and produce the most actionable answers.

  1. “What energy is blocking my connection with [name]?” — This invites specific insight about dynamics without demanding a prediction. Readers consistently give their best work with questions framed this way.

  2. “What do I need to understand about my pattern of choosing unavailable partners?” — Pattern questions are gold. They shift the focus from one person to your broader relationship landscape, which is where the real value lives.

  3. “What is [name] feeling about our relationship right now, and what is driving that?” — Specific, names a real person, asks for emotional insight plus motivation. This gives the reader two clear threads to pull.

  4. “What would a healthy next step look like in my situation with [name]?” — Action-oriented. You are asking for guidance, not a guarantee.

  5. “What am I not seeing about this relationship that I need to see?” — This takes courage to ask, but it produces some of the most valuable readings I have ever had. You are explicitly giving the reader permission to tell you something difficult.

  6. “How can I create space for a healthy relationship to enter my life?” — Great for people between relationships. It is forward-looking and puts agency back in your hands.

  7. “What does [name] need from me that I am not currently providing?” — Specific, growth-focused, and immediately actionable.

Tier 2: Solid Questions (Good Follow-Ups)

These work well but are slightly less open-ended. Use them after your Tier 1 questions.

  1. “What is the core lesson this relationship is trying to teach me?” — Insightful but can sometimes produce generic spiritual answers. Best paired with a Tier 1 question for context.

  2. “What energy do you see around my love life in the next few months?” — A reasonable timeline question that does not demand an exact date.

  3. “Is there something from my past that is affecting how I show up in relationships?” — Good for self-awareness, though be prepared for answers that might sting.

  4. “What do you sense about the long-term compatibility between me and [name]?” — Useful, but remember that compatibility readings are snapshots, not prophecies.

  5. “How does [name] truly view our future together?” — Decent third-party insight question. Works best when you provide the reader with context first.

  6. “What can I do to improve communication in my relationship with [name]?” — Practical and actionable. Some readers excel at this type of question.

  7. “What type of partner would be the healthiest match for me right now?” — Good for people who are actively dating and want to calibrate their radar.

Tier 3: Okay But Limited (Use Sparingly)

These questions are not terrible, but they tend to produce less useful answers.

  1. “Does [name] think about me?” — The answer is almost always yes, which makes it feel validating but not very useful. You already suspected the answer.

  2. “When will I meet my soulmate?” — Timeline predictions are the least reliable part of any reading. You will get an answer, but take it with a grain of salt.

  3. “Is [name] my twin flame?” — This invites label-based answers rather than substantive insight. Whether or not someone is your twin flame matters far less than whether the relationship is healthy.

  4. “Will my ex and I get back together?” — Not useless, but this yes/no framing limits what the reader can offer. Reframe it as: “What do I need to understand about the possibility of reconciliation with [name]?”

  5. “Are they being faithful to me?” — Readers can sometimes pick up on deceptive energy, but this is a question better answered by direct conversation or evidence. A reading should not replace your own judgment here.

Tier 4: Avoid These (Waste of Time and Money)

  1. “Tell me everything about my love life.” — This is not a question. It is an invitation for the reader to talk in circles for 30 minutes while your bill climbs.

  2. “Will he text me this week?” — Hyper-specific predictions about trivial actions are unreliable and not worth spending money on.

  3. “Should I text him first?” — You are paying a psychic to make a decision you can make yourself. This is a sign you should wait before booking a reading.

  4. “Is he the one?” — This question puts the reader in an impossible position. No one can or should answer this for you. Reframe it toward compatibility and growth.

  5. “Why are all men/women terrible?” — If this is where your head is at, you need a therapist, not a psychic. I say that with genuine compassion because I have been there.

The pattern is clear: the best questions are specific, name real people, focus on understanding rather than predicting, and leave room for the reader to share something you did not expect.

What Information to Have Ready Before Your Session

Walking into a reading without key details is like going to a doctor and refusing to describe your symptoms. You are making the reader work harder for no reason, and you are paying for those extra minutes.

The Essential Information Checklist

For love and relationship readings:

  • Full first names of everyone involved (yours and theirs)
  • Birth dates if you have them — many readers use astrology as part of their process
  • How long you have known each other or been together
  • The specific event or shift that triggered your desire for a reading
  • The last meaningful interaction you had with this person and when it happened

For career readings:

  • Your current job title and how long you have been there
  • Names of key people involved (boss, colleague, business partner)
  • Specific dates of events — when you applied, when the interview is, when the offer expires
  • What your gut is telling you (readers often confirm or challenge your intuition)

For general life direction readings:

  • Your age (or at least your general life stage)
  • The 2-3 biggest decisions currently on your plate
  • Any relevant deadlines
  • What you have already tried

What NOT to Share Upfront

There is a balance here. You want to give enough context for a focused reading, but you do not want to hand over your entire life story before the reader has said a word. A common tactic of less ethical readers is to let you talk for ten minutes, then feed your own information back to you repackaged as psychic insight.

My rule: share names, dates, and a one-sentence summary of the situation. Then let the reader lead. If they need more context, they will ask.

Mindset Preparation: How to Show Up Emotionally Regulated

This is the section that would have saved me the most money over the years. I cannot overstate this: do not book a reading when you are in crisis.

Why Crisis Readings Almost Always Disappoint

When you are sobbing, panicking, or seething with anger, three things happen that sabotage your reading:

  1. You cannot think clearly about what to ask. Your questions become reactive (“will he come back?”) instead of strategic (“what do I need to understand about this ending?”).

  2. You are more susceptible to hearing what you want to hear. A reader might offer a nuanced, balanced answer, but your brain filters it down to the one phrase that confirms your hope or fear.

  3. You will almost certainly go over budget. Emotional readings run long because you keep asking follow-up questions, seeking reassurance rather than insight. I have seen my own sessions double in length when I was not emotionally regulated going in.

The Emotional Readiness Test

Before you book, ask yourself these three questions:

  • Can I hear a difficult answer without spiraling? If no, wait.
  • Do I have specific questions written down, or am I just hoping to feel better? If the latter, call a friend instead.
  • Have I slept, eaten, and had water today? Basic needs sound irrelevant, but they directly affect your ability to process information.

If you cannot pass all three, give yourself 24-48 more hours. The reading will still be there. The reader is not going anywhere. Your situation will not become unsolvable because you waited two days to gain clarity.

Grounding Exercise (10 Minutes Before)

I do this before every reading now, and it has genuinely improved my results:

  1. Sit somewhere quiet with your question list in front of you.
  2. Take five slow breaths. In through the nose, out through the mouth.
  3. Read each question out loud to yourself.
  4. For each question, notice what you are hoping the answer will be. Write that hope down next to the question.
  5. Now consciously tell yourself: “I am open to an answer that is different from what I want to hear.”

That last step is the whole point. You are not trying to empty your mind. You are just creating a small gap between your expectations and reality so the reader’s actual insight has somewhere to land.

Technical Preparation: Chat vs. Phone vs. Video

The format you choose affects your reading more than most people realize. Each has real advantages and drawbacks, and the best choice depends on your personality and situation.

Chat Readings

Best for: People who process information by reading it, introverts, first-time clients who feel nervous about talking to a stranger, and anyone who wants a built-in transcript.

Advantages: You get a written record automatically. You can re-read the reader’s exact words later instead of relying on memory. You have more time to think about your responses. And honestly, some people just communicate better in writing.

Drawbacks: Slower pace means you cover less ground per dollar. Harder to pick up on tone and nuance. Some readers are better speakers than writers.

Pro tip: Both Kasamba and Keen offer chat readings. Screenshot your session as it progresses — do not rely on the platform saving it for you. I have lost transcripts before and regretted it.

Phone Readings

Best for: People who want a conversational flow, anyone dealing with complex emotional situations, and those who want to cover the most ground in the least time.

Advantages: Faster paced, more natural conversation, easier for the reader to pick up on vocal energy. You typically get more insight per minute compared to chat.

Drawbacks: No automatic transcript. Easy to forget key details after the session. Harder to stay on track with your questions.

Pro tip: Keep a notepad next to you and jot down key phrases and timestamps during the call. Immediately after hanging up, spend five minutes writing a summary while the details are fresh. Do not skip this step — your memory of a 30-minute reading will compress to about three sentences within 24 hours.

Video Readings

Best for: People who want the full experience, returning clients who have built rapport with a reader, and complex readings that benefit from visual energy work.

Advantages: The closest thing to an in-person session. The reader can pick up on your body language and visual energy. Feels more personal and connected.

Drawbacks: Not available on all platforms. Requires you to be camera-ready, which adds logistical friction. Can feel more intense than phone or chat for first-timers.

My Recommendation for Beginners

Start with chat. Seriously. I know it feels less “authentic” than a phone call, but the built-in transcript is invaluable when you are learning how to evaluate readers. You can go back and re-read exactly what they said, compare it against what actually happened, and start building your own sense of which readers deliver real insight versus generic reassurance. Once you have found a reader you trust, switch to phone for deeper sessions.

Budget Planning: How to Set a Hard Spending Limit

I am going to be direct about this because no one else in this space will be: psychic readings can become a money pit if you do not set boundaries before you connect. I have seen people — myself included, early on — spend hundreds of dollars in a single month chasing reassurance.

How to Set Your Limit

For first-time readings: Budget $20-$50. Both Kasamba and Keen offer introductory rates and free minutes for new users, which makes your first session significantly cheaper. Take advantage of these offers, but know exactly what the per-minute rate becomes after the promotional period ends.

For regular sessions: I recommend a monthly psychic budget, treated exactly like any other discretionary spending category. Mine is $60 per month. Some months I use it, some months I do not. But the cap exists, and I do not cross it.

For crisis moments: If you feel the urge to book a reading that would exceed your budget, that is actually a signal to stop. The desperation you feel is exactly the emotional state that produces the worst readings and the highest bills. Put your credit card in another room and revisit the decision in 24 hours.

Tactical Spending Tips

  1. Calculate your per-minute rate before you connect. If a reader charges $5/minute, a 20-minute session costs $100. Know this number.
  2. Set a timer on your phone. When it goes off, you are done. Thank the reader and end the session.
  3. Do not extend sessions impulsively. If you had three questions and got through two, book a follow-up session next week for the third. Do not let “just one more question” turn a $30 reading into a $90 one.
  4. Track your spending. I keep a simple spreadsheet: date, reader, platform, amount spent, and a one-sentence summary of the main takeaway. This does two things — it keeps you honest about your spending, and it helps you identify which readers consistently deliver value.
  5. Use introductory offers strategically. New to Kasamba? Use your free minutes to test a reader before committing to a full session. Keen also offers deals for first-time users. There is zero shame in being smart with your money.

The Red Flag Spending Pattern

If you find yourself booking readings more than twice a week, spending more than you would on a dinner out, or needing a reading before you can make any decision — please pause. This is not a judgment. I have been in that exact pattern. But at that point, the readings are functioning as emotional crutches rather than tools for insight. Consider talking to a therapist alongside — or instead of — your psychic practice.

Putting It All Together: Your Pre-Reading Checklist

Here is the complete checklist in order. Run through it before every session:

  • 48 hours out: Brain dump all your questions and concerns
  • 24 hours out: Refine to 3-5 specific, open-ended questions
  • 24 hours out: Choose your platform, reader, and format (chat/phone/video)
  • 24 hours out: Set your hard budget for this session
  • 30 minutes out: Do the emotional readiness test
  • 10 minutes out: Grounding exercise with your question list
  • At session start: Share names, dates, and a one-sentence summary
  • During session: Take notes or screenshot the chat
  • After session: Write a 5-minute summary while details are fresh
  • After session: Log the spending in your tracker

That is it. Ten steps. If you do even half of them, you will get dramatically better readings than the average person who connects on impulse.

Frequently Asked Questions

How should I prepare for my first psychic reading?

Start 48 hours before your session. Write down all your questions in a brain dump, then refine them to your top 3-5 the next day. Gather relevant details like names, birth dates, and specific dates of events. Set a firm budget — $20 to $50 is reasonable for a first reading. Thirty minutes before the session, do a quick emotional check-in and a brief grounding exercise. The single most important thing is to avoid booking during an emotional crisis. Wait until you can think clearly about what you actually want to know.

What are the best questions to ask a psychic about love?

The best questions to ask a psychic about love are specific, name real people, and focus on understanding rather than predicting. Questions like “What energy is blocking my connection with [name]?” or “What do I need to understand about my pattern of choosing unavailable partners?” consistently produce the most useful answers. Avoid yes/no questions like “Will he come back?” or “Does she love me?” — they produce vague answers and encourage you to keep booking readings for reassurance instead of actually processing the insight.

What should I expect from a psychic reading?

Expect a conversation, not a performance. A good reader will take a moment to tune in to your energy, may ask one or two clarifying questions, and then share insights that should feel relevant to your situation. Some information will resonate immediately, and some might not click until days or weeks later. Do not expect 100% accuracy, exact date predictions, or someone reading your mind word for word. The best readings feel like talking to someone who can see your situation from an angle you cannot access yourself.

How much should I budget for a psychic reading?

For a first reading, $20 to $50 is a reasonable range, especially when you factor in introductory offers from platforms like Kasamba and Keen. For ongoing sessions, set a monthly cap and treat it like any other discretionary budget. A well-prepared 20-minute reading with clear questions will deliver more value than an unfocused hour-long session at triple the cost. Always calculate the per-minute rate before you connect so there are no surprises.
