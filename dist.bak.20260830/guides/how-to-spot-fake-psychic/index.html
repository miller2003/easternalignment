How to Spot a Fake Psychic: 12 Red Flags From Someone Who Has Been Scammed

The psychic industry has real practitioners and real frauds operating side by side. After three years of testing readers across every major platform, here are the specific warning signs that separate genuine intuitive ability from calculated deception.

I have been scammed by a psychic.

I want to get that out front because I think it matters. I write about this industry professionally. I have tested over a hundred readers across multiple platforms over three years. I consider myself reasonably intelligent and appropriately skeptical. And I still got taken — not by a street-corner fortune teller with a neon sign, but by a reader on a legitimate platform who was skilled enough at the performance to bypass my defenses during a period of emotional vulnerability.

She cost me $340 and about six weeks of misplaced hope. I am sharing this because the number one thing that keeps people from recognizing psychic fraud is the belief that it only happens to gullible people. It does not. It happens to people who are hurting, and that is nearly all of us at one point or another.

This guide is the result of that experience and the systematic evaluation that followed. These are not abstract warning signs. These are the specific patterns I have observed in fraudulent readers — and the specific qualities that distinguish them from genuine practitioners.

The 12 Red Flags

Red Flag 1: They Ask More Questions Than They Answer

This is the single most reliable indicator of a fake psychic, and it is the easiest to spot in real time.

A genuine psychic leads with perception. They connect with your energy, the energy of the situation, or the energy of the person you are asking about — and they tell you what they perceive. They may ask for a name, a birth date, or clarification about who you are asking about. But the ratio of their statements to their questions should be heavily weighted toward statements.

A fake psychic reverses this ratio. They ask you to describe your situation. They ask what happened. They ask how you feel about it. They ask leading questions — “I am sensing something happened recently that upset you, is that right?” — that prompt you to provide the information they then feed back to you as insight.

The test is simple. In the first three minutes of a reading, count. How many statements of perception did the reader make without prompting? If the answer is zero or one, and you have been doing most of the talking, you are probably paying someone to listen to you and mirror your own narrative back.

Red Flag 2: Everything They Say Could Apply to Anyone

This is the cold reading baseline. Statements like:

  • “I sense you have been through a difficult period recently.”
  • “There is someone in your life who is not being entirely honest with you.”
  • “You have a lot of love to give but you are afraid of being hurt.”
  • “I see a major change coming in the next few months.”

Every single one of these statements is true of nearly every person who seeks a psychic reading. They sound specific because they touch on universal human experiences. They are not specific. They are broad enough to land with essentially anyone.

A genuine reader produces statements that are particular to you and your situation — details you did not share, dynamics you did not describe, descriptions of people or situations that you can verify as accurate.

Red Flag 3: The Curse, Dark Energy, or Spiritual Blockage Diagnosis

This is the most financially dangerous scam in the psychic industry, and it has been operating in essentially the same form for centuries.

The pattern: during your reading, the psychic tells you they have detected a curse, dark energy attachment, negative entity, or spiritual blockage that is causing your problems. Conveniently, they can remove it — for an additional fee. The fee is typically substantial, ranging from $100 to several thousand dollars.

After the “removal,” one of two things happens. Either the problem is declared solved and you leave feeling briefly relieved, or — more commonly — the curse is found to have “layers” that require additional sessions and additional payments to fully clear.

No legitimate psychic practitioner diagnoses a catastrophic spiritual problem and then charges to fix it in the same interaction. This is not a gray area. If you hear the word “curse” followed by a price tag, leave immediately.

Red Flag 4: Guaranteed Outcomes

“I guarantee he will come back to you.” “I can promise you will be married within a year.” “I guarantee this spell will work.”

No legitimate practitioner guarantees outcomes because outcomes involve other people’s free will, which no psychic can control. A genuine reader reads energy, identifies trajectories, and provides insight — they do not sell certainty.

A guarantee is a sales tool, not a spiritual practice. It is designed to exploit the desperation of someone who wants certainty badly enough to pay for it. And once you have paid for a guaranteed outcome, you are psychologically invested in believing it will happen, which makes you more likely to keep paying when it does not.

Red Flag 5: Pressure to Book Immediately or Urgently

“The energy window is closing. We need to act now.” “If we do not address this today, the opportunity will pass.” “I rarely see energy this urgent — we should schedule your follow-up immediately.”

Manufactured urgency is a sales technique, not a psychic impression. Energy does not operate on retail timelines. A genuine situation that requires attention will still require attention next week. A practitioner who creates artificial time pressure is managing your wallet, not your spiritual wellbeing.

Red Flag 6: Requests to Contact You Outside the Platform

If you are using a platform like Purple Garden, Kasamba, or Keen, any reader who asks you to contact them through a personal email, phone number, WhatsApp, or private website is either circumventing the platform’s payment protections or collecting your personal data for other purposes.

Platforms exist to protect both parties. The payment processing, the recorded session, the review system, and the dispute resolution — all of these evaporate when you move off-platform. Any reader who suggests this is prioritizing their own financial interest over your protection.

Red Flag 7: They Change Their Reading Based on Your Reaction

Watch for this carefully, because it is subtle. You ask about your relationship. The reader says “I see some distance between you.” You react with distress. The reader immediately softens: “But I also see a path to reconciliation — he is thinking about you.”

A genuine reader maintains their perception regardless of your emotional reaction. If they see distance, they describe distance and its implications — even if your face crumbles. A reader who modifies their statements in real time based on your visible emotional state is reading you, not the situation.

Red Flag 8: The Upsell Structure

Your reading reveals a problem. Conveniently, the reader also sells the solution — a candle, a spell, a spiritual bath, an energy clearing, a love binding. The reading was not really a reading. It was a diagnostic that exists to sell you the treatment.

Some practitioners genuinely offer additional spiritual services, and that is not inherently fraudulent. The red flag is when the reading consistently diagnoses problems that require the reader’s specific paid interventions. If every reading ends with a sales pitch for a separate service, the reading is a funnel, not a consultation.

Red Flag 9: Vague Timelines Delivered With Absolute Confidence

“He will reach out in the next two to three weeks.” Delivered with total certainty, no hedging, no acknowledgment of variables.

Genuine psychic perception of timing is inherently imprecise. Skilled readers know this and communicate accordingly. They speak in probability windows, they acknowledge that free will can alter timelines, and they hedge appropriately.

A reader who delivers specific timelines with zero uncertainty is telling you what you want to hear with a confidence level designed to prevent you from questioning it.

Red Flag 10: No Negative Information at All

This might be the subtlest red flag, and the one most easily missed because hearing only good news feels so pleasant.

If your reading contains zero uncomfortable truths — no challenges to address, no patterns to examine, no difficult information about the other person’s feelings or intentions — you are almost certainly being told what you want to hear rather than what is actually present in the energy.

Every real situation contains shadow. Every genuine reading addresses it, at least briefly. A reading that is entirely sunshine and confirmation is a performance, not a perception.

Red Flag 11: They Claim Powers No Legitimate Practitioner Claims

“I can make him fall in love with you.” “I can bring your ex back guaranteed.” “I can read anyone’s thoughts in real time.” “I can change the outcome of your situation.”

No legitimate psychic practitioner claims the ability to control other people’s feelings, override free will, or guarantee specific outcomes. These claims are not advanced abilities — they are lies. Any reader who makes them is fraudulent, regardless of their reviews, their reputation, or how confident they sound.

Red Flag 12: You Feel Worse After Every Session

This is the cumulative red flag — the one you notice in retrospect rather than in real time.

If you consistently feel more anxious, more dependent, and more confused after readings with a specific practitioner — not less — something is wrong with the dynamic. A legitimate reading should produce clarity, even when that clarity is uncomfortable. A fraudulent or incompetent reading produces anxiety because it feeds your fears while offering solutions that require additional payment.

Track your emotional state after readings. If a pattern of increased anxiety emerges, the reading is not serving you — regardless of how accurate individual statements may seem.

Cold Reading: The Technique Behind the Performance

Cold reading is a set of psychological techniques that allow someone with no psychic ability to create the appearance of intuitive knowledge. Understanding how it works is the single best defense against being fooled.

The Barnum Statement

Named after P.T. Barnum, these are statements vague enough to apply to almost anyone but specific enough to feel personal. “You have a tendency to be self-critical” — true of virtually everyone who seeks a psychic reading. “You have unrealized creative potential” — flattering and vague enough to be universally true.

The Fishing Technique

The reader throws out a broad statement and watches your reaction. “I am sensing a male energy around you — someone whose name might start with a J or an M.” There are enough men with J and M names in anyone’s life that this will almost always land. Your reaction tells the reader which direction to go.

The Rainbow Ruse

The reader attributes contradictory qualities to you: “You are generally confident, but there are times when you feel deeply insecure.” This covers every possible reality and sounds like genuine insight into your complexity.

How to Defeat Cold Reading

The defense is simple: give the reader nothing to work with. Do not nod. Do not elaborate. Do not confirm or deny. State your question and let the reader demonstrate their ability without your assistance.

A genuine psychic will still produce specific, verifiable perceptions. A cold reader will flounder.

Where to Find Legitimate Readers Instead

The good news: genuine practitioners exist. They are not the majority, but they are findable if you know what to look for.

Use established platforms with review systems. Purple Garden, Kasamba, and Keen all have review systems that allow you to evaluate a reader’s track record before connecting. Pay attention to reviews that describe specific accurate perceptions, not just reviews that say “loved my reading.”

Purple Garden is a good example of what a trust-first platform looks like in practice: readers introduce themselves on video before you spend anything, written reviews sit directly on each profile so you can look for the specificity patterns described above, and new users receive $30 in free credit — enough to test a reader against every red flag on this list without financial risk.

Use free or introductory minutes to vet. Do not commit significant money to any reader until you have tested their ability during a low-cost or free session. Kasamba’s 3-free-minutes-per-reader model is specifically designed for this purpose.

Trust specificity over style. A genuine reader may not have the most polished delivery or the most comforting bedside manner. What they will have is specific information you did not provide. Prioritize accuracy over aesthetics.

Trust your discomfort. If a reading included something you did not want to hear — something specific and uncomfortable — that is actually a positive signal. It suggests the reader is reading energy rather than managing your emotions.

FAQs

How can you tell if a psychic is real or fake?

The most reliable test is specificity without prompting. A real psychic provides specific perceptions — descriptions of people, dynamics, emotional states, or situations — that you can verify as accurate and that you did not share with them. A fake psychic asks you questions, makes broad statements that could apply to anyone, and adjusts their statements based on your reactions.

What should I do if I think I have been scammed by a psychic?

On a platform like Purple Garden, Kasamba, or Keen, report the reader through the platform’s feedback system. If you paid for a separate service (curse removal, spell work) that was sold through fear tactics, consider filing a complaint with your credit card company and your local consumer protection agency. Document everything — screenshots, receipts, session transcripts if available.

Are all psychics fake?

No. The industry contains genuinely skilled practitioners alongside fraudulent ones. The challenge is that both operate in the same spaces and sometimes use similar language. The red flags above help you distinguish between them. The most effective strategy is to use platform-based vetting, test with free minutes before committing, and prioritize readers who demonstrate specific perceptions over those who deliver only generalities and reassurance.

Can a psychic really read my mind?

Psychic perception is not mind reading in the science fiction sense. What skilled practitioners describe is the ability to perceive energetic states, emotional frequencies, and relational dynamics — not specific thoughts. A reader who claims to read your exact thoughts in real time is overstating their ability. A reader who accurately describes the emotional quality of a situation or the energetic state of a person you are asking about is demonstrating a more realistic — and more useful — form of perception.
