## The Evidential Standard: What Counts as Contact and What Does Not

Before the list, the test we applied — because "mediumship" covers two completely different products.

**Evidence** is specific, checkable, and was not provided by you: a name, a date, a physical description, an object, a private joke, the way someone signed letters. When Spirit Amy's clients report she described a person and a meeting date that "happened exactly," that is a checkable claim. When Quietsound gives you the actual names of the guides she says answered, that is a claim you can hold against future sessions.

**Comfort** is unfalsifiable: "he is at peace," "she watches over you," "they forgive you." It may be true, it may be what you need to hear — but no evidence distinguishes it from a script. A reading consisting entirely of comfort statements is worth its price as counseling, not as mediumship, and you should know which one you are buying before the meter starts.

One structural note in Kasamba's favor: the chat format removes cold-reading's raw material. A medium working by text has no voice tremor to read, no face to watch, no jewelry to interpret. Whatever specificity comes through a Kasamba chat session arrived there without the usual toolkit of the stage fake. That does not make every chat medium genuine — it makes the genuine ones easier to identify, because their evidence has no mundane explanation left.

## FAQs

### What is the difference between a psychic and a medium?

A psychic reads the energy of living situations — your relationship, your career, the intentions of the people around you. A medium claims contact with those who have passed, or with non-physical guides. Many readers do both, but the evidence standards differ: psychic claims get checked by events, mediumship claims get checked only by the specificity of information you did not provide. Our [full explainer](/guides/psychic-vs-medium-vs-tarot-reader/) covers which to book for what.

### How can I tell if a medium is cold reading me?

Cold reading feeds on information you supply — your tone of voice, your reactions, the details you volunteer. Kasamba's chat format already strips most of that. What remains is your typing: give only a first name and relationship, then evaluate what comes back. Vague warmth ("he is at peace") is unfalsifiable; specific, checkable details you never typed are not. The free 3 minutes exist to run exactly this test.

### How soon after a loss should I book a medium?

There is no correct interval, but there is a practical one. Sessions in the first raw weeks of grief tend to become paid crying — human and understandable, but expensive at per-minute rates and light on information. The readers on this list are at their most useful when you can hold a conversation: far enough from the loss to ask a real question, close enough that the question still matters. For immediate crisis support, a grief counselor is the right first call and a medium is the wrong one.

### Why are the top two mediums so expensive?
