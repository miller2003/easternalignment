Most people choose a psychic the way they choose a lottery ticket: based on the photo, the rating, and the hope. It is the worst decision process in an industry where the decision is everything, because the same platform that holds one excellent reader can hold five terrible ones beside it. The platform matters. The individual reader matters more.

I have tested hundreds of readers across the major platforms, and my selection process has distilled into a repeatable sequence. It is not mysterious. It is a combination of reading the right signals in the profile, mining the reviews the right way, using trial minutes as a deliberate test, and evaluating the first two minutes of the session. Anyone can do it, and doing it will put you in the top tier of clients that good readers actually want.

## Step 1: Start on Platforms With Accountability

The single biggest shortcut in choosing a psychic is choosing the platform first, because platforms differ dramatically in how much they let you verify before you pay.

The platforms I have tested in depth — [Keen](/reviews/keen/), [Kasamba](/reviews/kasamba/), and [Purple Garden](/reviews/purple-garden/) — all require advisors to earn reputation in public: un-deletable reviews, visible session histories, and in Purple Garden's case, a public outcome ledger called Journeys. On those platforms, a reader's record is researchable before you spend anything. On ad-driven hotline networks where the reader is only revealed after you are billed, you are gambling by design.

Within any platform, the same rule applies: never book a reader whose record you have not looked at. If you are on a platform where that is impossible, you are on the wrong platform.

## Step 2: Read the Profile for Specificity, Not Charm

The profile is where good readers accidentally tell the truth and bad readers accidentally expose themselves.

Good profiles are specific. They name their methods — "traditional tarot," "Lenormand cartomancy," "astrological synastry" — rather than vague claims of "connected to the spirit world." They define their specialties rather than promising everything. And notably, good profiles describe a limitation: "I do not do love spells," "I do not predict lottery numbers," "I will tell you the truth even when it is hard." The limitation is the signal, because a real practitioner knows what they do not do.

Bad profiles promise everything. They guarantee outcomes, they claim mastery of every discipline, they list paid add-ons and "energy clearings." If a profile reads like an advertisement for certainty, the reader is selling you something the industry cannot deliver — and my [guide on spotting fake psychics](/guides/how-to-spot-fake-psychic/) covers why that is the defining characteristic of the scam side.

## Step 3: Mine the Reviews Like a Professional

Star ratings are the least informative number in the industry, because they are aggregates of careers, not measures of current quality. The actual information lives in the individual reviews, and the way to extract it is to hunt for specificity.

Ignore reviews that say "she was so sweet" — they tell you about warmth, not ability. Hunt for reviews that say "she accurately described my situation without me telling her" or "she named the month and it happened." Specific claims are checkable; emotional compliments are not. Then sort by recent and read the last ten, because a reader's current quality is what you are buying, not their career average.

The other review signal is the rebound: how the reader responds to a negative review. A reader who attacks critics in the replies has told you how they will treat you when you disagree with them. A reader who responds with professionalism has told you the same. Both are information, and both are free.

## Step 4: Use Trial Minutes as a Test, Not a Reading

Every major platform offers cheap or free trial minutes — Keen's 5 minutes for $1, Kasamba's 3 free minutes per new reader, Purple Garden's $30 credit — and the correct use of them is not to get a reading. It is to run a test.

The test has three parts. First, state your situation in two sentences and ask your one question — the preparation protocol from my [questions guide](/guides/questions-to-ask-a-psychic/). Second, be silent and observe: does the reader deliver specific, unprompted detail quickly, or do they spend the trial asking you questions and building rapport? Third, judge the cadence: a good reader produces substance fast, because their skill is real; a slow reader is burning your money on pleasantries.

The trial is the closest thing the industry has to a free sample, and it is the only reliable way to evaluate the connection before committing real money. Use it ruthlessly.

## Step 5: Judge the First Two Minutes of the Paid Session

The same test applies when the paid session begins, because the quality of the connection does not change at the trial boundary. In the first two minutes, a genuine reader offers at least one specific, unprompted impression — a detail about your situation, a physical sensation, a name-adjacent impression. If the first two minutes are entirely questions from the reader, you are being cold read, and the polite move is to end the session and keep the remainder.

The reason this works is structural. A reader with real ability cannot help but demonstrate it, because the perception is the work. A reader without ability fills the space with questions and generalities because that is all they have. The distinction is visible in minutes, and it is the same distinction that separates every good session I have ever had from every bad one.

## Step 6: Audit After the Session

The final step happens in the weeks after, and it is the one most people skip. When a reader makes a prediction with a window — "communication within two weeks," "a job offer around the 18th" — write it down. When the window passes, check it. That single habit tells you more about a reader's real accuracy than every review on the platform, because it is evidence you collected yourself.

This is why I consistently recommend chat readings with permanent transcripts for the readers you are evaluating long-term: the transcript is the audit trail. My [guide on predictions that missed](/guides/psychic-prediction-didnt-come-true/) covers what to do when a reader is wrong, and the answer is not automatically to abandon them — every reader misses sometimes — but the pattern of hits and misses over time is the truth about the reader, and it is the truth you are paying for.

## The Bottom Line

Choosing a good psychic is not a lottery. It is a process: accountable platform, specific profile, mined reviews, deliberate trial, tested first minutes, and an honest audit afterward. Each step removes a layer of risk, and together they turn what most people experience as a gamble into a selection.

The [platform reviews on this site](/reviews/) and the [individual reader reviews](/reviews/keen/) are the result of me running this exact process hundreds of times, and they are the shortcut if you do not want to do the work yourself. But the process is worth learning regardless, because it is the difference between being the client good readers want and the client bad readers exploit.
