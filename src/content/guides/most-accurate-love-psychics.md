## The Five Verification Types (So You Can Audit Anyone)

"Accurate" is the most abused word in this industry. Every profile claims it; almost none can prove it. After tracking prediction outcomes across 150+ reviewed readers on three platforms, we can tell you that real accuracy evidence comes in exactly five forms — ranked here from strongest to weakest. Use this taxonomy on any reader, on or off this list.

**Type 1 — Third-party hostile verification.** An independent, skeptical audience tracks the predictions and confirms them. The strongest evidence that exists, because the verifier has no incentive to be kind. One reader on this list has it.

**Type 2 — Public-ledger confirmation.** Clients document the prediction in a permanent public record (Purple Garden's Journeys) and return to confirm or contradict. The reader cannot edit the record. Strong because it is falsifiable and permanent.

**Type 3 — Client-maintained logs.** A client keeps their own written record of predictions across months or years and reports the hit rate. Strong because the log predates the outcome — no retroactive fitting.

**Type 4 — Delayed verification.** The client's initial reaction is rejection ("she was wrong, I was furious") followed weeks or months later by confirmation. Strong because initial resistance rules out the reader telling the client what they wanted to hear.

**Type 5 — Outcome-verified closed cases.** The prediction concerned a decided matter (a declined application, a finished relationship) and the outcome reversed as predicted. Strong because the situation was closed — the prediction was the only variable that changed.

Anything below these five — "I felt she was accurate," "she knew things," a five-star rating left minutes after the session — is not accuracy evidence. It is satisfaction evidence. Both matter; only one belongs in an accuracy ranking.


Verification works differently on each platform: Kasamba's review feeds, Keen's completed-session counts, and Purple Garden's [Journeys prediction ledger](/reviews/purple-garden/). Each produces a different class of evidence, which is why the ranking below separates them.

### Why do accurate psychics sometimes get the timing wrong?

Because timing is the highest-variance element of any prediction. A reader can correctly see the trajectory — the reconnection, the offer, the decision — while the date flexes with the free-will choices of everyone involved. Treat timing as the least reliable element of any reading and the structural read (what is happening, which way it bends) as the reliable core. The readers on this list have the strongest documented timing records available — and even their records include slips.

### Can I test a reader's accuracy before paying for a full session?

Yes, and the protocol is free. Give the reader a first name and one sentence of context — nothing more — and evaluate what they produce unprompted. Specific, checkable statements you did not feed them are the signal; intake questions are the anti-signal. Kasamba's 3 free minutes per new advisor, Keen's 5-minutes-for-$1, and Purple Garden's $30 credit all exist to run exactly this test. Then, when the prediction comes, write it down with the date and check it when the window closes — that is how you build your own Type 3 log.

### Is a more expensive psychic more accurate?

Not per dollar — but there is a pattern at the extremes. Sustained premium pricing ($15+/min) is a survival filter: readers whose accuracy does not justify the rate get removed by the market within months, so everyone who survives at that tier for years has passed a hard test. The reverse is not true at the budget end: several $2–$4/min readers (Sparks Of Insight, lorrie c) hold accuracy records that embarrass premium readers. Accuracy and price correlate at the extremes, not in the middle.
