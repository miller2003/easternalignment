## The Audition Protocol: What the 3 Free Minutes Are Actually For

Everything on this list hangs on one discipline, so it comes before the rankings.

The 3 free minutes are not a free sample of your reading. They are a **verification window** — your chance to find out whether this reader connects to your situation before you pay. Used correctly, they are the highest-value minutes in the session. Used as a free sample, they produce three minutes of throat-clearing and a paid session that starts cold.

The protocol:

1. **Open with the skeleton, not the story.** "I'd like a read on my relationship with [first name] — we've been in no contact for six weeks." That is all. Do not narrate the history, do not explain what you hope is true. Everything you volunteer is information the reader no longer has to produce.
2. **Then be quiet and evaluate.** A strong reader, given only the skeleton, starts producing specifics you did not supply — his emotional state, the shape of the silence, something checkable. A weak reader spends the window asking you questions. That difference is the entire audition.
3. **Decide at minute three.** If the free window produced something specific and checkable, stay and let the meter run — you have verified connection. If it produced questions and generalities, politely end it and audition the next reader on this list. That is what the free window is for. Walking away costs nothing.

For the complete strategy — including the silence test and how to chain multiple auditions — read [our Kasamba 3 free minutes strategy guide](/guides/kasamba-3-free-minutes-guide/).

## Five Rules for a First Reading That Is Not Wasted

1. **Write the question first.** One sentence, specific, with a first name if it involves a person. "Will I find love" produces a horoscope; "what is actually happening between me and [name] since the silence started" produces a reading.
2. **Run the audition, do not skip it.** The 3 free minutes are for verification — skeleton only, then silence and evaluate. The reader who asks you questions for three minutes has told you what the paid thirty would look like.
3. **Set a hard budget before you open the chat.** $20–$25 for a first session. Every reader on this list can deliver inside that ceiling; a reader who needs more of your money to answer your question is not answering your question.
4. **Stay in chat, not voice.** Transcripts are your receipts — you will re-read this session in three weeks to check what was actually said. Voice readings evaporate. (The exception: a reader whose voice rate is cheaper than chat, which exists but is rare.)
5. **Judge the reading in three weeks, not three minutes.** Note the specific, checkable claims — dates, descriptions, "he will reach out by" — and check them when the window closes. That check, not the feeling in the moment, is how you find your reader for the long term.

## FAQs

### How much should a first psychic reading cost?

On this list, $15–$25 for a complete first session after the new-client discount. The math: 3 free minutes for verification, then 8–12 paid minutes at $1.99–$3.99/min with 50% off the first session. If a first reading costs $100, either the reader is premium-tier (a choice, not a default) or the session was unstructured (a mistake). Start cheap, verify, then decide if the premium tiers are worth it for your question.

### Is 3 minutes really enough to evaluate a reader?

Enough to evaluate connection, not to get your answer. In three minutes a strong reader can take a one-sentence skeleton and produce two or three specifics you did not provide — a personality read, a dynamic, a detail about the silence. That is the signal you are paying for. If the three minutes produce intake questions instead, you have also learned something, for free.

### Should I tell the reader it is my first time?

Yes — but say only that. "This is my first reading, and my question is [one sentence]" tells the reader to calibrate their pacing without handing them a script. What you should not do is narrate your situation's full history upfront: every detail you volunteer is one the reader no longer has to produce, and production is the test.

### What if my first reading turns out to be wrong?

Check what "wrong" means first. Timing slips are the most common miss — the outcome lands, the date does not; that is an accuracy limitation of the entire field, not necessarily a bad reader. Structural misses are different: if the core read of the situation was off, you have learned a $20 lesson about that reader, and the 3-free-minutes-per-new-advisor structure means your next audition starts at zero again. One wrong reading is data. The protocol — verify free, pay once, check at three weeks — keeps the tuition low.
