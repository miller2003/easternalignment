## What "Details and Solutions" Means as a Contract

Advisor Maya's service title isn't decoration — it's her format. Her profile opens with terms most readers bury or omit:

> *"I will not judge you and I will not sugarcoat your reading so please feel free to ask anything you like. I do not provide time frames."*

That's three promises in two sentences: **judgment-free** (the awkward questions are welcome), **unsweetened** (the answer will be the answer), and **timeline-free** — the last of which we'll defend in a moment, because it's more honest than it first appears. And then the line that separates professionals from performers:

> *"If you are unhappy with your reading by any chance, please give me the opportunity to make it right with you before leaving any negative review."*

She's telling you, before you spend a dollar: if a session misses, she fixes it first. That's a service guarantee written by someone who has resolved thousands of sessions and expects to resolve thousands more.

## The One Honest Caveat — and Who It's Actually a Warning For

Two caveats, both from her own terms, stated plainly.

**She doesn't do time frames.** If your burning question is "when" — when will he call, when will it end, when do I move — she will redirect you to what and how. That's a methodological choice (dates in this trade are guesses wearing costumes), but if you need a date to function, book a timeline-forward reader instead; the platform has them.

**She doesn't sugar coat.** If tonight's version of you needs the gentle telling, her directness will land as brisk. She is compassionate — sweetintuition's review is the proof — but her compassion comes wrapped in honesty, not instead of it. Fragile nights deserve softer instruments; save her for the nights you can use the truth.

## Should You Book Advisor Maya?

**Book her if** you've had enough of vague comfort and want the detailed, unvarnished, solution-shaped answer — from a nine-year veteran who promises in writing to make it right if she misses, at a price where the $30 credit does the risking for you.

**Skip her if** you need dates and deadlines from your readings, or if tonight calls for cushioning over clarity. Both are legitimate; neither is her format.

Either way, ten free minutes are sitting in that credit. Ask her the question you've been re-wording for weeks. If Hadee's experience is the pattern — and 9,680 thumbs suggest it is — the part that "seems inaccurate" at minute two may be the part that's most right by the end.
