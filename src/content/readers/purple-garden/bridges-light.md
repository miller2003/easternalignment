Some readers arrive at tarot by leaving the world behind. Bridges Light arrived by running the world — for twenty years, as a **corporate leader** — and then choosing the cards on purpose.

Her bio tells it without varnish: *"I have had involvement with the Tarot and my spirit guides for over 19 years... I was a Corporate Leader for 20 years and am now fulfilling my purpose in the next leg of my journey as a full time Tarot Reader, Coach and Guide."*

That combination matters more than any adjective on her profile. Twenty years of management teaches you to hear what a situation actually is beneath what's being said about it — and it teaches you to deliver a hard truth with a next step attached. Her session style is the fusion: intuitive clairvoyance and mediumship for the seeing, coaching discipline for the *so what*. The platform has noticed: **34,415 readings since 2021**, a **perfect 5.0**, and the **highest_rated badge** — computed from paying clients, not publicity.

## The Reading Style: Direct, Structured, Unsweetened

Her published terms are refreshingly blunt for a profile page: *"I relay the details that come through directly which means I do not sugar coat the information."* She also asks for what actually helps her work — relevant context, plus the first name and date of birth for any person of interest — a request that reads less like form-filling and more like a professional intake.

Her specialty map is explicit: **love, relationships, career and finance**, with the rare honesty of stated limits — she doesn't take legal or health questions. On her sister-app profile (Purple Ocean, run by the same company, where she carries the same reputation), she even publishes her working hours: 11am to 8pm, seven days. Most psychics make you guess; she treats availability like an executive treats a calendar.

## The Price, Translated Into What You're Actually Buying

Bridges Light prices almost flat across modes — **$4.99 chat, $5.49 voice, $5.99 video** — the smallest mode-gap we've measured on Purple Garden. You can pick the format that fits your question instead of your wallet.

Purple Garden's new-client offer — **$30 free credit on your first purchase** — covers roughly six free chat minutes, or about 60% of a 15-minute session (~$75 cash). For a perfect-5.0, badge-holding reader, that's the cheapest the door has ever been.

One tactical note from her own intake request: send the first name and birthday of the person you're asking about. Her readings lean precise — names and dates are how she locks on — and clients who arrive with those get the deeper read sooner.

## The One Honest Caveat — and Who It's Actually a Warning For

The caveat is printed in her own terms: **no sugar coating.** If your situation needs the truth dressed in reassurance — the "everything happens for a reason and he'll be back by spring" softening — you'll find her directness bracing. She's kind; she isn't soft. For clients stuck in loops of vague readings, that's precisely the product. For clients who need gentleness first, book one of Purple Garden's cushion-style readers and come back when you're ready for the plan.

Second: volume and published hours mean the **queue is real**, and she notes candidly that request volume can prevent her from answering every follow-up comment. Set expectations accordingly — and use the notify-me button rather than refreshing at 2 a.m., when she's definitionally offline.

## Should You Book Bridges Light?

**Book her if** you want a reading that behaves like great consulting — accurate intake, direct findings, a plan you can execute this week — from a reader whose 5.0 survived thirty-four thousand chances to crack, and whose two careers give your question both the sight and the strategy.

**Skip her if** you're seeking the softest voice in the app tonight, or if you need a reader outside her stated lanes (legal, health). Neither is a flaw; both are fit questions.

Either way, the credit does the risking: $30 free, near-flat pricing, and a five-star ledger behind it. Send the name, send the birth date, ask the real question. If matt's review is the norm — and 9,592 thumbs say it is — you'll get the answer before the timer, not after.
