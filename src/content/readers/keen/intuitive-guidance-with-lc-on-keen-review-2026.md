## The "Fairytale Reader" Concern: What the Forum Said and What the Evidence Actually Shows

Any honest review of Intuitive Guidance with LC has to address the characterization that appeared in independent forum discussion: *"super sweet but may be a fairytale reader"*. I am going to give this its full weight rather than dismissing it, because it describes a specific failure mode that is worth understanding — and then I am going to show you why the evidence, taken as a whole, does not support the characterization as her dominant mode.

### What "Fairytale Reader" Means — and the Specific Signal That Raises It

A fairytale reader is a psychic advisor who prioritizes client comfort over honest accuracy — who tells clients what they hope to hear, frames difficult situations as more positive than they are, and maintains high ratings by generating emotional satisfaction rather than genuine insight. The mechanism is seductive: clients who are told what they want to hear leave happy reviews, the rating stays high, and the advisor builds a reputation built on warmth rather than truth. The concern is raised by Lori's compassionate style and high warmth indicators in her review language — and it is reinforced by a specific pattern in some recent reviews. The phrase *"I hope her predictions come to pass"* appears in the record. Hope-language rather than confirmation-language in a review is a mild flag: it suggests the client received predictions they wanted to believe rather than information they had independently verified.

### Why 7-Year Repeat Clients Are the Most Direct Answer to This Concern

The most powerful counter-evidence to the fairytale reader concern is not any individual review. It is the documented existence of clients who have been returning to Lori for seven or more years. One client states explicitly: *"I have been working with Lori since 2018"*. A client who returns across seven years — through the full arc of a life during which predictions either materialized or did not — is not returning for flattery. They are returning because the guidance has been useful and the predictions have been sufficiently accurate to sustain trust across time. Fairytale readers can sustain a first session and sometimes a second. They rarely sustain seven-year client relationships, because the gap between what they promised and what unfolded becomes impossible to ignore across that timeframe. The existence of these long-term relationships does not eliminate the fairytale concern entirely — it is still worth monitoring your own experience for aspirational versus accurate delivery. But it substantially reweights the evidence toward genuine competence rather than comfortable performance.
