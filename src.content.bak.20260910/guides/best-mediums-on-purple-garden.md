Mediumship earns its bad reputation honestly. It is the one psychic specialty where the product — contact with someone who has passed — cannot be falsified in the moment it is delivered, which makes it the natural home of every fraud in the industry. The standard defenses available to clients are weak: star ratings measure how the session felt, and grief makes everything feel meaningful.

Purple Garden accidentally built the two strongest anti-fraud structures in online mediumship. The first is the **Journeys ledger**: clients document what a medium told them and return weeks later to confirm or contradict — publicly, permanently, in a record the medium cannot edit. Comfort claims evaporate there; specific claims get tested. The second is **live video**: a medium working on camera has their entire process visible — their eyes, their pauses, their hands. Cold reading depends on managing what the client sees; video hands the client the full feed.

This list ranks seven mediums by evidential structure: blind-test protocols, Journeys records, repeat-client behavior in grief contexts, and lineage credentials where they exist. One of them operates under a rule that makes cold reading structurally impossible — he refuses to hear your backstory at all. ## The Evidential Standard, Applied to This Platform

Before the list, the three tests we applied to every candidate — the same standard as our cross-platform medium rankings, tuned to Purple Garden's tools:

**Specificity that could not be guessed.** Names, dates, physical descriptions, private references — produced before you supply them. Journeys is where these claims get logged and checked; we weighted readers with documented, dateable, confirmed specifics above everyone else.

**Refusals.** Real mediums decline categories: no health diagnoses, no legal outcomes, no death dates. A reader who answers everything is telling you nothing they say is checked. Every reader on this list has stated limits; we read their profiles for them before we read anything else.

## FAQs

### Is video worth the premium for a mediumship session?

More often than in any other specialty — for two reasons. Mediumship with photographs or objects needs the shared visual reference that only video provides, and grief sessions are precisely the emotionally complex conversations where being seen changes the quality of the reading. The exception is a simple, specific question — "is there anything unresolved with my father" — where chat delivers the same information at half the price.

### How do I know a medium is not reading my reactions?

On chat, give minimal input — name and birthdate — and evaluate what comes back unprompted. On video, the test inverts: the medium can see you, so the burden shifts to protocol. Tattooed Psychic's no-backstory rule is the strongest answer on the platform; every other reader on this list works from minimal intake if you hold the line on what you volunteer.

### What should I not ask a medium?

Death dates (yours or anyone's), health diagnoses, and legal outcomes — every legitimate medium refuses these, and the refusal is the credential. Also avoid "what does the future hold" open-endedness; grief readings work best anchored to a specific relationship and a specific question.

### Why do the generational claims (4th, 5th, 7th generation) matter if they cannot be verified?

On their own, they do not — a lineage claim is unfalsifiable marketing. What makes them meaningful here is the pairing: a claimed seven-generation practice that also survives 40,001 sessions of public review is consistent with technique absorbed over a lifetime. Read the lineage as the explanation, not the evidence; the session ledger is the evidence.
